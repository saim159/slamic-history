Historical evidence

How to read historical sources

A research-oriented history website should distinguish surviving evidence, later interpretations and modern scholarship.

Primary sources

Texts, documents, inscriptions, coins and other evidence connected to the period under study.

Historical chronicles

Compare authors, dates, intended audiences and the circumstances in which accounts were written.

Modern scholarship

Use scholarly books and peer-reviewed research to compare interpretations and trace citations.

A useful habit: Record the author, title, date, edition or translation, page number and a stable link whenever available. Do not treat every historical report as independently verified fact.
