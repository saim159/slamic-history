02 · Research pathways

Choose a question. Follow the evidence.

Each pathway opens a different window into the past. Start with a theme, then develop a focused research question.

PATHWAY 01

Knowledge & Libraries

Explore libraries, madrasas, manuscript culture, translation and the institutions that preserved and transmitted knowledge.

Research question
How did institutions support the circulation of knowledge?
PATHWAY 02

Philosophy & Ideas

Study philosophical debates, theology, ethics, logic and encounters between different intellectual traditions.

Research question
How did scholars respond to competing ideas?
PATHWAY 03

Science & Medicine

Investigate astronomy, mathematics, optics, medicine and the historical methods used to produce knowledge.

Research question
How did observation and experimentation change scientific practice?
PATHWAY 04

Trade & Cities

Explore ports, markets, caravan routes, crafts, currencies and the economic relationships linking regions.

Research question
How did trade shape urban life and cultural exchange?
PATHWAY 05

Society & Everyday Life

Examine family, education, food, clothing, social class, religious practice and ordinary people's experiences.

Research question
What can everyday objects reveal about past societies?
PATHWAY 06

Environment & Travel

Study travel writing, geography, climate, water systems, pilgrimage and how landscapes shaped human activity.

Research question
How did geography influence movement and settlement?
03 · A broad chronological guide

History unfolds across many periods

These are broad historical signposts, not strict boundaries. Different regions experienced change at different times.

7TH–8TH CENTURIES

Formative Period

The emergence of early Muslim communities and new political and cultural networks.

8TH–13TH CENTURIES

Intellectual Networks

Translation, scholarship and scientific activity developed in multiple centres.

13TH–16TH CENTURIES

Regional Transformations

Political changes, new dynasties and continuing intellectual and commercial exchange.

16TH–19TH CENTURIES

Early Modern Worlds

Large empires, shifting global connections and new institutional developments.

04 · Research practice

From curiosity to a credible historical argument

Good historical research distinguishes evidence from interpretation and asks who created a source, when, why and for whom.

A practical workflow

  • Define a focused, answerable research question.
  • Identify primary sources and relevant scholarly studies.
  • Compare accounts, dates, terminology and perspectives.
  • Record citations and explain uncertainty honestly.
  • Build a conclusion that follows from the evidence.

Explore types of evidence

📜
Written sources

🏺
Material evidence

🗺️
