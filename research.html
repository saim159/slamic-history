Research Pathway 05

Themes & Research Questions

History is not only a record of what happened. It is also an investigation into why events happened, how people understood their world, and what the surviving evidence can actually tell us.

Explore by theme

Six fields of historical inquiry

These are starting points for future research, not claims that every question has already been answered. Each topic can become a separate, referenced research article.

THEME 01

Knowledge, Education & Libraries

Investigate how knowledge was taught, preserved, translated and circulated across different periods.

Questions to investigate
  • What roles did libraries and book markets play?
  • How did scholars access and transmit texts?
THEME 02

Philosophy, Theology & Intellectual Debate

Explore debates about reason, revelation, ethics, knowledge and the nature of reality.

Questions to investigate
  • How did different thinkers define the role of reason?
  • How did intellectual disagreements change over time?
THEME 03

Science, Medicine & Mathematics

Study scientific practices, instruments, medical texts, mathematical methods and the movement of ideas.

Questions to investigate
  • What methods did historical scientists use to test ideas?
  • How did texts and techniques travel between regions?
THEME 04

Trade, Cities & Economic Life

Explore markets, maritime routes, taxation, crafts, urban institutions and regional connections.

Questions to investigate
  • How did trade connect distant communities?
  • What evidence reveals everyday economic life?
THEME 05

Society, Family & Everyday Life

Examine social groups, family structures, work, urban life and the experiences of ordinary people.

Questions to investigate
  • Whose experiences are preserved in historical sources?
  • What can legal documents tell us about social life?
THEME 06

Environment, Geography & Travel

Study climate, water, landscapes, travel writing, pilgrimage routes and geographical knowledge.

Questions to investigate
  • How did geography shape settlement and travel?
  • How reliable are historical travel accounts?
Research principle: A question is not a conclusion. Test historical interpretations against sources, consider alternative explanations and clearly distinguish evidence from speculation.

A practical research workflow

01. Define the question Narrow the topic by period, place, person or historical problem.
02. Locate the evidence Find primary sources and reliable modern scholarship. Record full references.
03. Compare and explain Compare accounts, assess limitations and support conclusions with evidence.
