Seljuk Civilization

السَّلَاجِقَةُ
11th–12th Centuries

The Seljuks were a major Turkic dynasty that played an important role in the political, military, cultural and intellectual history of the medieval Islamic world.

1. Origins of the Seljuks

The Seljuks emerged from the Oghuz Turkic world of Central Asia. Their rise gradually transformed the political landscape of large parts of the Islamic Middle East.

The dynasty became especially influential during the eleventh century, when Seljuk rulers expanded their authority across Iran, Iraq and Anatolia.

2. Tughril Beg and the Rise of Seljuk Power

Tughril Beg was one of the most important early Seljuk rulers. He established Seljuk political authority in Iran and later entered Baghdad.

In 1055, Tughril entered Baghdad and became an important political protector of the Abbasid caliphate while exercising substantial political authority himself.

3. The Abbasid Caliphate and Seljuk Authority

The Seljuk period illustrates an important distinction between religious legitimacy and political authority in medieval Islamic governance.

The Abbasid caliph continued to hold important symbolic and religious significance, while Seljuk sultans exercised much of the practical political and military power.

4. Alp Arslan

Alp Arslan succeeded Tughril Beg and became one of the most important Seljuk rulers.

His reign is particularly remembered for the expansion of Seljuk influence and the Battle of Manzikert in 1071 against the Byzantine Empire.

5. Battle of Manzikert

The Battle of Manzikert was fought in 1071 between the Seljuk forces led by Alp Arslan and the Byzantine army led by Emperor Romanos IV Diogenes.

The Seljuk victory had major long-term consequences for the political and demographic history of Anatolia.

6. Malik Shah I

Malik Shah I, who ruled from 1072 to 1092, presided over the high point of the Great Seljuk Empire.

During his reign, the empire experienced considerable political stability, territorial expansion and intellectual development.

7. Nizam al-Mulk

Nizam al-Mulk was the famous vizier of Alp Arslan and Malik Shah. He played a major role in Seljuk administration and political organization.

He is also associated with the development of the Nizamiyya network of institutions of higher learning.

8. The Nizamiyya Madrasas

The Nizamiyya madrasas became important institutions of Islamic learning in the Seljuk period.

They supported the study of Islamic law, theology, Arabic language and other scholarly disciplines.

The Nizamiyya of Baghdad became especially famous, and the scholar Abu Hamid al-Ghazali taught there for a period.

9. Al-Ghazali and the Seljuk Intellectual World

Al-Ghazali's career was closely connected with the intellectual environment of the Seljuk period.

His appointment to teach at the Nizamiyya of Baghdad placed him at one of the most prestigious centres of learning in the Islamic world.

10. Persianate Culture

The Seljuk political world developed within a strongly Persianate cultural environment.

Persian was widely used in administration and literature, while Arabic remained central to religious scholarship and many intellectual disciplines.

11. Administration

Seljuk rulers inherited and developed administrative traditions from earlier Islamic and Iranian political systems.

Viziers, provincial administrators, military commanders and various bureaucratic institutions helped govern the large territories under Seljuk authority.

12. The Iqta System

The iqta was an important institution in medieval Islamic political and military administration.

Under the Seljuks, forms of iqta administration were used to assign revenue rights connected with military and administrative responsibilities.

13. Seljuk Military Organization

The Seljuk state depended heavily on cavalry and military elites drawn from the wider Turkic and Islamic military world.

Military organization allowed the Seljuks to maintain authority across large and geographically diverse territories.

14. Architecture

Seljuk architecture is one of the most visible parts of the dynasty's cultural legacy.

Mosques, madrasas, caravanserais, tombs and other structures reflected the combination of Iranian, Islamic and Turkic architectural traditions.

15. Seljuk Mosques

Seljuk religious architecture contributed to the development of mosque design in Iran and surrounding regions.

The four-iwan mosque plan became particularly important in the architectural traditions of the region.

16. Madrasas and Scholarship

The Seljuk period witnessed the expansion of organized institutions of Islamic learning.

Madrasas helped provide structured education in law, theology, language and related scholarly disciplines.

17. Science and Intellectual Life

The Seljuk era existed within a much broader Islamic intellectual environment in which scholars worked in astronomy, mathematics, medicine, philosophy, law, theology and literature.

Intellectual activity was not limited to the Seljuk court but developed through networks of scholars, libraries, madrasas and cities.

18. Omar Khayyam

The famous Persian mathematician, astronomer and poet Omar Khayyam lived during the Seljuk period.

He participated in astronomical and calendrical work and is associated with the reform of the Persian calendar under Malik Shah.

19. Seljuk Anatolia

Seljuk political influence in Anatolia developed into the Sultanate of Rum.

Cities such as Konya became important centres of political, commercial and intellectual life.

20. Caravanserais and Trade

Seljuk rulers in Anatolia supported infrastructure that helped facilitate long-distance commerce.

Caravanserais provided places where merchants and travellers could rest and protect their goods along important routes.

21. Sufi and Spiritual Culture

The medieval Seljuk world witnessed the growth of diverse spiritual and Sufi traditions.

The intellectual and spiritual environment of Anatolia later produced figures such as Jalal al-Din Rumi, whose life and writings became deeply influential in Persianate Islamic culture.

22. The Sultanate of Rum

After the weakening of the Great Seljuk Empire, Seljuk political authority continued in Anatolia through the Sultanate of Rum.

Its rulers contributed significantly to the development of Anatolian cities, architecture, trade and Islamic culture.

23. Decline of the Great Seljuk Empire

After the death of Malik Shah I in 1092, internal political struggles weakened the unity of the Great Seljuk state.

Competition among rulers, regional powers and military elites contributed to the fragmentation of Seljuk authority.

24. Mongol Expansion and the Later Seljuks

The expansion of Mongol power in the thirteenth century transformed the political landscape of the Middle East and Anatolia.

The Sultanate of Rum eventually came under Mongol influence before its political structure fragmented.

25. Seljuk Legacy

The Seljuks left a lasting legacy in political institutions, architecture, education, Persianate culture and the history of Anatolia and the Middle East.

Their period also helped shape the political environment from which later Muslim states, including the Ottomans, emerged.

26. Seljuk Timeline

1037: The Great Seljuk state traditionally dates its establishment to this period.
1055: Tughril Beg enters Baghdad.
1071: Battle of Manzikert.
1072: Malik Shah I becomes sultan.
1092: Death of Malik Shah I.
12th century: Seljuk political fragmentation and the growth of regional successor states.
13th century: Mongol expansion transforms the political order of Anatolia.

27. Why the Seljuks Matter

The Seljuks are important for understanding the transformation of the medieval Islamic world after the Abbasid political order weakened.

Their history connects political power, military organization, Islamic education, Persianate culture, architecture and the development of Anatolia.

28. Key Ideas to Remember

  • The Seljuks were a major Turkic dynasty of the medieval Islamic world.
  • Tughril Beg entered Baghdad in 1055.
  • Alp Arslan's victory at Manzikert in 1071 had major consequences for Anatolia.
  • Malik Shah I presided over the high point of Great Seljuk power.
  • Nizam al-Mulk played a major role in Seljuk administration and educational institutions.
  • The Nizamiyya madrasas became important centres of Islamic learning.
  • Seljuk architecture influenced the later architectural traditions of Iran and Anatolia.
  • The Sultanate of Rum continued Seljuk political traditions in Anatolia.

29. Further Research

Students interested in Seljuk history can explore the political relationship between the Abbasid caliphate and Seljuk sultans, the Nizamiyya educational network, the iqta system, Seljuk architecture, the Battle of Manzikert and the development of the Sultanate of Rum.
