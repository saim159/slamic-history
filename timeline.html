Islamic History Timeline

Follow the major turning points, political transformations, intellectual developments, and historical milestones of the Islamic world across the centuries.

A Journey Through Time

Islamic history spans more than fourteen centuries and includes diverse societies, dynasties, intellectual traditions, and geographical regions. This timeline highlights selected milestones that help us understand the broader historical development.

c. 610 CE

The Beginning of Revelation

According to Islamic tradition, the first Qur'anic revelation was received by Prophet Muhammad ﷺ in the Cave of Hira near Makkah.

Significance: The beginning of the prophetic mission and the revelation of the Qur'an.
622 CE

The Hijrah

Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and his followers migrated from Makkah to Madinah. The migration became a foundational event in Islamic history.

Significance: The Hijri calendar is traditionally dated from this event.
632 CE

End of the Prophetic Era

Prophet Muhammad ﷺ passed away in Madinah. Abu Bakr was subsequently recognized as the first caliph of the Rashidun Caliphate.

Significance: Beginning of the period of the Rightly Guided Caliphs.
661 CE

The Umayyad Caliphate

The Umayyad dynasty established its political center in Damascus and governed a vast territory stretching across several regions of the Islamic world.

Significance: A major transformation in the political history of the early Islamic world.
750 CE

The Abbasid Revolution

The Abbasids came to power and established a new caliphate. Baghdad later became one of the most important intellectual and commercial centers of the medieval world.

Significance: The beginning of the Abbasid political era.
909 CE

The Fatimid State

The Fatimid dynasty established itself in North Africa and later expanded into Egypt, where Cairo became a major center of political and cultural life.

Significance: The development of Cairo as an important center in Islamic history.
1258 CE

The Fall of Baghdad

Mongol forces captured Baghdad, bringing the Abbasid caliphate in Baghdad to an end after centuries of political and intellectual prominence.

Significance: A major turning point in the political and intellectual history of the medieval Islamic world.
c. 1299 CE

The Rise of the Ottoman State

The Ottoman polity emerged in northwestern Anatolia and gradually developed into a major empire spanning parts of Europe, Asia, and Africa.

Significance: Beginning of a political tradition that would last for centuries.
1453 CE

Conquest of Constantinople

Sultan Mehmed II captured Constantinople, which subsequently became the Ottoman imperial capital and was widely known as Istanbul.

Significance: A major turning point in Ottoman and Byzantine history.
1492 CE

The End of Muslim Granada

Granada, the last major Muslim-ruled polity in Iberia, surrendered to the forces of Ferdinand and Isabella.

Significance: Marks the end of Muslim political rule in the Iberian Peninsula.
1922 CE

End of the Ottoman Sultanate

The Ottoman Sultanate was formally abolished in 1922 during the political transformation that followed the First World War.

Significance: The end of the Ottoman sultanate and a major transition into the modern political era.

History Continues

The timeline does not end with the fall of an empire. Islamic history continues through modern states, intellectual movements, educational institutions, cultural traditions, and the lives of millions of people across the world.
