How should we study the distant past?

The ancient past cannot be reconstructed from one kind of source alone. Historians compare written accounts with inscriptions, material remains, environmental evidence and the context in which each source was produced.

Research principle

Distinguish religious belief, a later historical report, a contemporary inscription and a modern scholarly interpretation.

Research Pathways

Six areas of investigation

01

Human Origins and Sacred History

Study how religious traditions describe creation and early humanity.

Research question: What kind of question can each source answer?

02

Mesopotamia and Early States

Explore cities, writing systems, trade networks, kingship and the evidence left by communities around the Tigris and Euphrates.

Research question: How do inscriptions and material remains complement chronicles?

03

Ancient Egypt and the Nile

Investigate the relationship between geography, agriculture, state power, monumental architecture and religious practice.

Research question: What can monuments tell us, and what can they not tell us?

04

Ancient South Arabia

Study Saba and other South Arabian kingdoms through inscriptions, settlements, irrigation systems and trade.

Research question: How do inscriptions help reconstruct a kingdom's history?

05

North Arabia and Regional Networks

Examine settlements, caravan routes, neighbouring empires and changing political and cultural links.

Research question: How did trade connect communities and empires?

06

Arabia Before Islam

Explore tribal society, poetry, religion, markets and regional political alliances without reducing the whole region to one uniform society.

Research question: What do different sources reveal about the diversity of Arabia?

Chronology

A preliminary historical framework

The periods below are broad guides, not a complete or universally agreed chronology.

c. 8000–2000 BCE Early communities, agriculture and changing settlement patterns.
c. 3500–1000 BCE Urban development, writing traditions and political states.
1st millennium BCE South Arabian kingdoms and expanding evidence from inscriptions.
1st–6th centuries CE Arabian communities interacted with neighbouring powers.
Research Practice

Questions every researcher should ask

  • Who produced this source, and approximately when?
  • Is it contemporary with the event, or was it written later?
  • What was the author's purpose, audience and political context?
  • Can another independent source support or challenge the claim?
  • What remains uncertain, disputed or unknown?
