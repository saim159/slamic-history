Abbasid Baghdad

762–1258 CE
بَغْدَادُ العَبَّاسِيَّةُ

Baghdad became one of the most important political, intellectual and cultural centres of the medieval Islamic world. The Abbasid period witnessed major developments in translation, medicine, mathematics, astronomy, philosophy, literature and historical scholarship.

Al-Andalus

711–1492 CE
الأَنْدَلُسُ

Al-Andalus refers to the territories of the Iberian Peninsula under Muslim rule at different periods. It became an important centre of urban life, scholarship, philosophy, medicine, agriculture, architecture and intercultural exchange.

Ottoman Civilization

c. 1299–1922 CE
الدَّوْلَةُ العُثْمَانِيَّةُ

The Ottoman Empire developed into a major political and cultural power spanning parts of Europe, Asia and Africa. Its legacy includes architecture, law, administration, education, arts and scholarship.

Fatimid Civilization

909–1171 CE
الدَّوْلَةُ الفَاطِمِيَّةُ

The Fatimid state established Cairo as its capital and developed an influential political, intellectual and architectural tradition in North Africa and Egypt.

Mamluk Civilization

1250–1517 CE
دَوْلَةُ المَمَالِيكِ

The Mamluk Sultanate played a major role in the political and military history of the medieval Middle East. Cairo became a major centre of scholarship, architecture, trade and religious learning.

Seljuk Civilization

11th–12th Centuries
السَّلَاجِقَةُ

The Seljuks played an important role in the political and intellectual development of the medieval Islamic world. Their period is associated with institutions of learning, architecture, administration and the growth of Persianate culture.
