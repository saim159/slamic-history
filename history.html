History of Islam

A chronological introduction to the major periods, people, events and civilizations that shaped Islamic history.

A Journey Through History

Islamic history spans more than fourteen centuries and encompasses a vast geographical area. It includes not only political history, but also scholarship, culture, law, philosophy, science, art and social development.

c. 570–632 CE

The Prophetic Era

The history of Islam begins in seventh-century Arabia with the life and mission of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. According to Islamic tradition, the first revelation came to him in the Cave of Hira around 610 CE.

The Beginning of Revelation

The Qur'an began to be revealed to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ through the Angel Jibril. The early message emphasized tawhid, accountability, moral responsibility and devotion to Allah.

The Hijrah

In 622 CE, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and his companions migrated from Makkah to Madinah. This migration, known as the Hijrah, became a defining event in Islamic history and later formed the starting point of the Islamic calendar.

The Madinan Community

In Madinah, a new Muslim community developed with religious, social and political institutions. The Prophet ﷺ continued his mission until his death in 632 CE.

632–661 CE

The Rashidun Caliphate

Following the death of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali رضي الله عنهم successively led the Muslim community. These four caliphs are traditionally known as the Rashidun, or Rightly Guided Caliphs.

Expansion and Administration

During this period, Muslim rule expanded beyond Arabia into parts of the Byzantine and Sasanian territories. The period also witnessed important developments in administration, governance and the preservation of Islamic knowledge.

661–750 CE

The Umayyad Caliphate

The Umayyad Caliphate established its capital at Damascus and governed a vast territory stretching across parts of Asia, Africa and Europe.

Expansion

During the Umayyad period, Muslim territories expanded significantly. Islamic rule reached the Iberian Peninsula in the early eighth century, while expansion also continued eastward into Central and South Asia.

Architecture and Culture

The Umayyad period produced important architectural monuments and contributed to the development of Islamic artistic and administrative traditions.

750–1258 CE

The Abbasid Era

The Abbasids came to power in 750 CE and established Baghdad as their capital. Baghdad developed into one of the major intellectual and cultural centers of the medieval world.

The Translation Movement

Scholars translated and studied works from Greek, Persian, Indian and other intellectual traditions. This contributed to a broader culture of scholarship and inquiry.

Science and Scholarship

Mathematics, medicine, astronomy, philosophy, geography and other fields developed through the work of scholars working across the Islamic world.

711–1492 CE

Al-Andalus

Muslim rule was established in much of the Iberian Peninsula beginning in the early eighth century. Over the centuries, Al-Andalus became an important region of Islamic political, intellectual and cultural history.

Córdoba

Córdoba became a major center of learning, architecture, literature and intellectual activity. The region produced influential scholars and thinkers whose works circulated beyond Iberia.

The End of Muslim Granada

In 1492, Granada, the last major Muslim polity in Iberia, surrendered to Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile.

c. 1299–1922

The Ottoman Empire

The Ottoman state emerged in Anatolia and gradually became one of the major empires of the early modern world. At its height, its territories extended across parts of Europe, Asia and Africa.

Constantinople / Istanbul

In 1453, Ottoman forces under Sultan Mehmed II captured Constantinople. The city became an important political, cultural and economic center of the empire.

Legacy

Ottoman history includes major developments in architecture, administration, scholarship, diplomacy and urban culture. The Ottoman Sultanate was abolished in 1922.
